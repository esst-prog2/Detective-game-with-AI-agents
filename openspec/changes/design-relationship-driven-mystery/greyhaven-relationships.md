Victim edges: Mara is Adrian's sister and habitual peacekeeper; Owen his nephew and disputed fund custodian; Iris his estranged daughter and unauthorized copier; Ruth a trusted friend and bereaved petitioner; Jonah a service provider who disliked Adrian's obstruction of inquiries. All have access to the station. A social role is not a permission to know Adrian's private actions.

## The emotional engine of the mother-son lie

Mara sees Owen leave the chart room angry, with a dark red sleeve, then refuses her questions. When Adrian is found dead she connects those observations through fear. She did not see a blow and does not know whether the stain is blood. Her statement that he stayed with her is a conscious lie, not faulty memory. His own omission is separate: an unauthorized repair did not authorize taking the money. The player should be able to hold both facts without making either a murderer.

The first change is evidential: the later living observation contradicts the implication Mara drew from the visit. The second is relational: independently corroborated work gives her a reason to stop protecting a feared act and explain the decision. Neither a respectful adjective nor a high cooperation value establishes those facts. M1 can be reached through a firm comparison or evidence-grounded reassurance; M2 requires corroborated repair as well. See [gate table](greyhaven-disclosure-design.md).

After M1 she can still avoid saying why she lied. After M2 she may say, "I was so busy trying not to lose him that I stopped listening to him." This is proposed optional emotional speech, not evidence that she knew his innocence all along. Owen's reply, if his O1 and her M2 have both been delivered and explicitly raised with him, can acknowledge hurt: "I needed you to ask me, not invent me somewhere safer." No off-screen reconciliation event or auto-shared statement is implied. Initial scope can omit that extra reply; the recorded accounts already carry the emotional consequence.

Aggression may make either guarded or terse but cannot destroy the independent route. The detective never has to promise immunity, lie about evidence, side with the family, or endorse the misuse of money. The correct accusation does not absolve Adrian's suppression, Owen's spending, Iris's copying or Mara's obstruction. Exposing unrelated harm is part of the story rather than a reward penalty system.

## Relationship limits

Knowing Ruth cared for Mara authorizes public history, not knowledge of Ruth's intent. Knowing Iris sent copies authorizes the shared source, not a conspiracy. Jonah's record cannot turn Mara's fear into a fact; Owen's innocence does not make every word of his first account complete. No NPC learns another's revision just because the player heard it. Any cross-account question must explicitly present the already committed statement through a reviewed comparison.

## Revised trust and investigation consequence

Ruth's 32-year connection predates the historical wrong. She distrusted the board while continuing practical care for Mara/Owen and accepting Adrian's specific no-receipt assurance; the recent receipt, not an arbitrary mood change, breaks that explanation. Family trust explains why folder storage is initially ordinary, not why the player should accept her alibi. She exploits that trust after a deliberate escalation and lets Owen bear suspicion, complicating her claim to protect the household.

The repair explains an independent encounter: a manual pressure test needed Jonah as observer, so Owen saw him answer the direct house telephone. Owen can say this at O0 without naming the caller or admitting the money. O1 supplies the financial reason and prearranged test context, helping the detective reinterpret evasion as fear of a real breach of trust. Mara's lie remains about feared violence, not knowledge of an innocent repair; her correction never acquits Ruth by itself.

After M1/M2 the household has lost a reassuring story. The next questions concern the still-sealed original, later voice/exit and distinction between collecting and storing it. Resolving one family's protective deception opens those questions rather than settling them. No NPC automatically learns another's revised account.
