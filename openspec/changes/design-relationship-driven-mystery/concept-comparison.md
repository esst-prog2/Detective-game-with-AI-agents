## Recommendation: A, The Last Bell at Greyhaven

Recommend A for review, **not selection or approval**. It most directly demonstrates the requested distinction: a mother can know about a visit, suspect her son, and lie to protect him while he is innocent of this murder. Resolving that lie should reshape the investigation rather than end it. The small cast makes independent routes, useful clarification and an unpredictable offline demo realistic.

B is the best alternative if the user prefers workplace/financial conflict and interpretation of records over family drama. C is the strongest later candidate if source reliability and a shared past are the priority and more writing time is available. There is no numerical quality score or claim of measured player preference.

## Preliminary arc for A, not a full timeline

An initial family accusation makes Owen plausible. The player distinguishes the visit from the killing, exposes the limitation of the shared time reference, and finds an independent explanation for one suspicious trace. Mara can revise a protective account without becoming an omniscient witness. The investigation then needs converging motive/access/behavioral evidence about Ruth; her private backstory cannot substitute for proving opportunity. Exact events, offsets, clues, testimony and secrets are deliberately left for post-selection design.

## Feasible first playable scope (proposed ceilings, pending approval)

For A, propose five living interviewees plus the victim, five static purposeful rooms, roughly eight inspectable clues, three non-murder secret seeds and one protective relationship central to the investigation. Keep at most two revised accounts and two authored cross-account comparisons in the initial version. Author about eight targeted clarification responses for the important ambiguities, rather than five universal follow-ups for every sentence.

Retaining the present six base topic slots would require 30 base responses for five NPCs. Allowing roughly four disclosure/revision responses and eight clarification responses puts the first authored speech budget near 42 bodies, before a small amount of guidance/comparison framing. This is a budgeting estimate, not a complete utterance inventory or implementation commitment. Start each new body with one exact approved fallback; expand delivery alternatives after narrative correctness. If meaningful base answers cannot justify all 30 slots, decide explicitly whether a case-scoped sparse-topic contract is worth the added validation work; do not fill the catalog with repetitive padding.

Essential deductions need alternative source routes, but a route can reuse an inspected document or an already-authorized witness observation. It need not double every speech body. The later proof graph must show which sources are genuinely independent; two witnesses repeating one record are one underlying source.

## Effort and risk budget

Indicative effort for one developer: 2-3 focused sessions for the selected story's timeline/permissions/graph and independent test oracles; 3-5 for the bounded policy/clarification extensions and boundary tests; 3-5 for content and cross-interface integration; 2-3 for adversarial/route testing and first-time-player revision. Approximately 10-16 focused sessions, not calendar promises. Available deadline and session length are unknown; if time is tight, reduce optional variants/subplots before essential route redundancy or safety review. B and C would likely exceed this scope unless simplified.

## Minimum versus later expansion

Minimum: one selected case alongside preserved Ashcroft, authored policy selecting current testimony, explicit belief-versus-observation references, statement-specific clarification, bounded disclosure records, two ways to reach essential discoveries, private evidence-aware endings, complete offline play.

Later: sixth/seventh interviewees, moving schedules, general NPC-to-NPC conversations, evolving social graphs, numerical trust/affection simulation, many endings, persistence, AI-generated facts, new model agents, voice and extensive art. None is assumed necessary for the first richer case.

## Decisions for creative approval

Choose A, B or C, or request a revision; approve setting/tone and the broad culprit/motive premise before it becomes canon. Confirm whether the protective mother should be uncertain about her innocent son's involvement (recommended) or knowingly protect a guilty son (a different moral structure needing a revised proof graph). Approve scope/content budget and identify the available semester/demo time. Then approve full story planning, not implementation, as the next step.
