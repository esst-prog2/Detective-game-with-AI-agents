## The promise

Rain needles the windows of Greyhaven's old lifeboat station, now a modest guesthouse. Retired magistrate Adrian Vale has gathered family and friends before depositing his papers with the county archive. At the delayed ferry's departure bell, two men leave the quay to collect his document box. They find him dead in the chart room. His sister says her son never left her. The son refuses to explain the money he argued over. The woman everyone trusts offers tea and practical help.

The player reconstructs what each person actually observed, rather than deciding whose nervousness looks like guilt. Exposing the mother's lie removes a seemingly solid alibi but opens an innocent explanation. Establishing that explanation matters emotionally even though it does not identify the murderer. The last movement of an old deposition, and a bell that rang twice, turn a family argument into an investigation of someone else's carefully maintained account.

## True history and why tonight

In 2008 a ferry capsized outside Greyhaven, killing two passengers. Ruth's brother Elias Bell was its master. A harbor engineer's sworn statement described an unmarked breakwater obstruction and recorded that the harbor board had overruled Elias's request to suspend the service. Before the 2009 trial, Adrian received the original statement. He withheld it to protect the board chairman, his late wife's brother, from exposure. Elias was convicted of negligent manslaughter on the incomplete record and died in prison in 2012. The missing statement would support reopening the conviction; it is not a magic document that settles every legal issue.

Adrian retained the date-stamped original with his own receipt annotation as leverage against the chairman. The chairman died in September 2026. Adrian now wants a tidy retirement deposit without that liability. Three days before the gathering, Iris found Adrian's signed pretrial receipt and a reference to the missing statement while sorting papers, and privately sent Ruth receipt/correspondence copies. Ruth had long believed Elias was scapegoated; this was her first concrete evidence that the original reached Adrian personally, contrary to his assurance. It did not yet establish his deliberate choice to suppress it. They had known each other since 1994 through clinic and harbor volunteering. Ruth cared for his wife in 2004 and Mara after surgery in 2019, and taught Owen first aid. After Elias's conviction she distrusted the board but had accepted Adrian's claim that the original never reached him. The copied signed receipt challenged that explanation; it did not yet prove why he withheld it. Care for the family remained real even as trust in Adrian became strained.

Ruth asked for the original and a signed acknowledgment before the archive handover the following morning. Adrian scheduled a private meeting at 20:37, after the expected ferry departure. At their 20:18 tea visit he offered clerical-error wording, leaving her hope of an honest account at the later meeting. Only then did he privately acknowledge a conscious choice and make release conditional on her withdrawing the petition; if she refused, the original would not enter the archive and he threatened its destruction. His reason for retaining it had ended; the imminent deposit made a decision unavoidable. This is why Ruth confronts him now rather than years earlier.

That same handover explains the other disputes. Adrian demanded repayment of Owen's unauthorized spending from the entrusted guesthouse maintenance fund before the accounts went with the papers. Iris wanted the family records preserved independently rather than curated by her father. The secrets converge because of a shared deadline, not five unrelated crises happening by chance.

## The killing and concealment

Owen's earlier argument concerns £480 he redirected from an entrusted guesthouse maintenance fund to repair the community rescue launch's cooling pump without permission. It was useful work but a real breach of trust. He planned to replace the money from unpaid invoices; the repair and the money problem are distinct from murder. Red marine primer on his sleeve and his angry departure frighten Mara, who has already lost a husband suddenly and mistakes concealment for impending catastrophe.

Ruth came seeking the original and a signed account, not carrying a murder plan. At20:36 Adrian calls the ferry office about collecting the archive box; near 20:37 Jonah hears him address Ruth and a second familiar voice answer. This is auditory identification, not a witnessed killing. At20:37 Ruth enters the chart room for the scheduled meeting. Adrian is demonstrably alive after Owen's visit. At 20:38 he refuses an honest acknowledgment and turns to retrieve the papers for destruction. Ruth deliberately strikes him with the heavy bronze chart weight from the desk. This is an intentional, angry act of retribution, not an accident excused by grief. He falls, overturning the ink bottle. At 20:39 Ruth tears the numbered paper seal on the grey deposition folder and takes it. She leaves the weight on the floor. At 20:40 she exits through the terrace door, carrying the folder under a folded shawl. Iris sees her face and the folder edge; neither Iris nor Mara sees a blow.

Ruth reaches reception at 20:41, wraps the folder in a dry cloth and puts it on the communal linen cupboard's lower shelf, routinely used for dry archive overflow. Mara, beside the linen table, reads 41/Bell original before the cover is wrapped. Ruth chooses familiar shared storage to separate the original from her person while preserving it and buying time. This is temporary concealment; she has no realistic long-term plan once the body is found. Her nursing bag is on a reception chair, not beside the evidence; no bag-proximity clue identifies her. Mara cannot certify the cupboard after the alarm. Ruth admits later storage but claims she collected the folder at 20:18, before the first bell. That false timing conflicts with independent observations of the still-sealed original on Adrian's desk at 20:36. It also conflicts with her claim to have stayed in reception between the bells.

Jonah and Owen discover the body at 20:43. The scene suggests fatal blunt violence; the chart weight is a possible implement, never a forensically established weapon. No player-facing confession, blood identification, fingerprint match or precise medical death time is proposed. The solution remains converging circumstantial evidence.

## Adrian beyond the crime

Adrian was generous with useful favors: he found forms for widows, checked Owen's invoices and funded repairs whose dedication plaques he quietly corrected. He remembered anniversaries but treated apologies as drafts he could edit. Iris misses the father who taught her to read charts and resents the adult who controlled which stories counted. Mara remembers a protective older brother; Jonah remembers petitions answered politely without action. Ruth's betrayal hurts because the kindness was real alongside the deliberate suppression. His private final admission is developer truth, never an NPC confession or an ending fact. Public characterization emerges through ordinary relationship answers, the scheduled accounting and the curated archive, not an opening lecture.

## Public opening, pacing and ending

Opening briefing, proposed public text: "Adrian Vale has been found dead in the chart room. The ferry has just left. Five people remain at the station. Please establish what they saw, inspect the accessible rooms, and confirm an accusation only when you are ready; confirmation ends this investigation."

Do not open with the historical conviction or a list of hidden secrets. Initial room descriptions mention the archive crates, damp ferry notice and unfinished pump work. The first leads are the disturbed scene, Owen's argument, Mara's unusually firm alibi and the fact that people use 'the bell' differently. Early dialogue can be warm, irritated or practical without indicating guilt.

Mid-investigation, the ferry record moves the second bell to 20:42 and opens a twelve-minute interval previously flattened by ordinary language. Owen's repair activity and a live sighting/call show his argument preceded the killing. Mara can concede what she saw and then why she lied. Iris's research packet reframes Ruth's grief and Adrian's reputation. The folder's movement matters only after the player distinguishes a planned appointment, an actual visit and a false account of its timing.

The ending uses only recorded inspections and delivered testimony. A supported accusation explains the obtained opportunity/custody contradictions and an independently corroborated living-after-Owen boundary, with uncertainty. A correct guess or partial chain says the investigation did not establish substantial support; it does not fill missing history, clock facts or relationships. Incorrect accusations stay private and final, revealing neither Ruth nor an undiscovered solution. Any emotional coda about Mara or Owen requires their relevant accounts to have been obtained. No score, deduction board or every-clue requirement.
