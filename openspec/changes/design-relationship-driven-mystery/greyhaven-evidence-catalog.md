# Greyhaven locations and evidence catalog

> APPROVAL BASELINE (2026-11-06): This narrative is approved as the baseline for technical implementation planning. Earlier proposal/creative-review wording below records design history, not a remaining baseline approval gate. Human fairness/readability acceptance remains open; production implementation is not authorized.

**Proposed developer plan; no live objects or interfaces added.** G1-G8 are eight inspectable investigation items. Components within an item are a single situated inspection, not hidden extra mandatory clues. All are available from the start under the constable's scene-inspection authority; no consent minigame, theft mechanic or hostile-interview lock. Public inspection descriptions omit developer conclusions and private motives.

## G1 — disturbed chart-room desk

**Form/find:** inspect the scene in C: Adrian beside a fallen chair, a bronze chart weight on the floor, spilled blue ink, and the retained stub of a paper band marked 41. **Proposed public text:** "The desk has been disturbed. Ink spreads across a rectangular clean patch where something lay. A paper-band stub marked 41 is caught beneath the blotter. The heavy chart weight lies on the floor beside the chair. Nothing here gives a precise time or identifies who handled it."

The same desk inspection shows a fixed corded house handset labeled "Ferry office—direct line"; G3 shows its fixed partner labeled "Chart room—direct line". This ordinary two-handset connection explains call origin; no hidden switchboard, voice recorder, speakerphone, caller-ID trick or specialist telephone knowledge. **Objective:** disturbance, missing rectangular item, numbered stub, possible implement and visible house-line endpoint. **Misreading:** Owen works with heavy tools, therefore used this object; weight definitely killed Adrian; ink proves exact freshness. None authorized. **Explanations:** no NPC can identify the actual blow; Iris/Jonah can supply last-living bounds. **Use:** connects G6's torn band to the desk and supports disturbed-removal context; no forensic 'perfect tear match' claim. Required in proposed route B's contextual support, optional in route A. Alternative: I-F's observation of seal 41 on the original supplies custody context without G1. No ending may reveal the true weapon.

## G2 — archive handover schedule

**Form/find:** R table, one ordinary list in Adrian's handwriting: "20:25 Owen—pump accounts; 20:35 Iris—family papers; 20:37 Ruth—Bell original and petition; 09:00 tomorrow—county archive." A routine margin note says dry archive overflow goes on the lower linen shelf. This is a plan. Iris did not keep her 20:35 appointment; Ruth falsely says she did not keep hers. **Objective:** intended meetings and shared deadline, not attendance. **Misreading:** the last scheduled visitor must be the killer, or a listed time proves presence. **Explanations:** everyone can explain their own listed business, never another's hidden actions. **Use:** makes questions about multiple disputes discoverable; optional for both sufficient routes. Ruth's admission of a proposed meeting remains available without inspecting it. No essential fact relies on deciphering initials or legal jargon.

## G3 — ferry watchbook and departure notice

The fixed house-line handset at the open hatch is labeled "Chart room—direct line"; G1 exposes the matching fixed endpoint. These labels are visible room facts, not a late technical reveal. **Form/find:** F desk/board, a single inspection of the day's service record: normal printed departure 20:30; posted delay due mooring snag; "20:30 signal, held at berth"; "20:36-20:37 Vale: collect box after meeting; hold for morning archive collection"; "20:42 second signal, departed". **Objective:** what the operator recorded, ordinary schedule and that night's exception. The work instruction/time are contemporaneous notes. The book does not record the folder wording, Ruth's name or the background exchange; it is an ordinary operator's task log, not an audio transcript. J-C can supply these details immediately on a direct question; they are not hidden behind inspection. Log and recollection share one author, so timing is corroborated by his own record, not an independent witness. **Misreading:** two entries mean two ferries; every witness meant the second peal; a timetable independently proves every minute. **Explainer:** Jonah's J-B/J-C distinguish his direct operation/recognized voice from reported folder position. **Use:** essential to both published routes' timing interpretation; J-B alone can supply the mechanism if a player misses the record, but future supported-ending rules require either inspected G3 plus J-B or a reviewed full J-B-recordless account. The latter is not part of the current minimum routes and must not be silently assumed in tests.

## G4 — pump pressure-test worksheet

**Form/find:** B bench, ordinary sheet initialed by Owen/Jonah at start 20:32 and finish 20:41, pressure readings, red-primer work note and parts receipt referencing £480 maintenance-fund payment. **Objective:** recorded work/material/payment, not a tamper-proof alibi. Hatch/bench layout is directly inspectable here. **Misreading:** paint proves every stain is innocent, or signatures prove uninterrupted work without testimony. The parts receipt is dated 16 October, and a before-supper note agrees a witnessed test during the evening departure period; a mooring delay leaves Jonah at the hatch while crew clear the snag. The rescue pump is separate from ferry propulsion: Owen neither caused the aborted signal nor secretly repaired the ferry. **Explanations:** Owen explains work; Jonah J-P says he watched the test while at the hatch, turned toward the phone for seconds, and saw no departure. **Use:** G4 + J-P + G3 substantially reduces Owen's opportunity after Adrian's later survival; J-P separately covers mutual presence from the 20:41 test finish through the 20:43 discovery, so the sheet is not stretched beyond its recorded interval; C is about a minute's walk from F, so his recollection of a seconds-long look away does not support a full round trip and assault. This is human corroboration, not a measured impossibility. O0 independently saw Jonah answer the phone without hearing the caller; the unauthorized purchase explains the real work and why this observation exists. No stopwatch conviction. Core to both minimum routes and M2; alternative support can later be designed, but currently do not claim G4-free justification. Owen's secret can be left unresolved while his work is corroborated.

## G5 — terrace sightline

**Form/find:** stand at T's sheltered bench and inspect lit glazed C door and route to R. The large exterior ferry-office clock faces the terrace and quay; ordinary room prose already mentions it. Public description shows the desk and a large black number on a white sealing band would be visible at about two metres when someone stands at the door; the inner R-C entrance is hidden by a chart rack. At the sheltered bench, the recessed door's angle hides the desk itself while the threshold remains visible under a porch lamp. At the crime-night observation Iris checked that clock, walked to the glazed door to ask about records, then returned to the bench; she was not reading small print across a courtyard. **Objective:** what could be seen from these positions, not that anyone historically saw it. **Misreading:** terrace witnesses could see the blow or every visitor. **Explainer:** Iris's I-F acknowledges precisely that blind spot. **Use:** required to obtain reviewed detailed visual identification I-F for route A; optional for route B. J-C supplies an alternative last-living/sealed-original source; M-S plus G6 an alternative custody source. Inspecting G5 never creates a sighting by itself.

## G6 — recovered grey deposition folder

**Form/find:** R communal linen cupboard's lower dry overflow shelf under a cloth, used for archive packets during sorting. This storage convention is visible in ordinary reception prose/G2, not revealed only after suspicion. Ruth's bag is on a chair and supplies no ownership shortcut. Constable's authority, not invasion prompted by guessing culprit. Folder bears title "Bell deposition—original", torn paper band 41, official pretrial receipt stamp and Adrian's signed receipt note; a blue ink smear crosses its edge. Pages contain the engineer's statement and the signed pretrial receipt. No draft confession, private last-meeting threat or petition-withdrawal demand lies helpfully beside them. G7 contains the earlier correspondence about clerical-error wording; R-H can explain the grievance directly. **Objective:** original exists, pretrial receipt, incomplete seal and current shared-cupboard position. Ink is compatible with G1 but neither dates removal nor identifies a hand. **Misreading:** a shared cupboard identifies its user; stamp proves deliberate suppression or a killer; access proves guilt. Number 41 is an ordinary archive reference repeated on the cover/band end, not a unique forensic tear match. **Explanations:** Ruth R-F claims she collected it 20:18; Mara M-S directly read 41/Bell original on the cover and saw later placement, without reading the pages; Iris I-F saw same numbered sealed folder at desk; Jonah J-C reports Adrian's folder statement and recognizes two voices after 20:30. Voice recognition supports presence; it does not visually identify the folder. **Use:** central physical custody/document strand in both routes. No unique personal accessory and no confession note. The earlier-copy G7 cannot substitute for this original's location/seal. Two alternative testimony sources relate this physical object to its later movement, not two magically interchangeable physical clues.

## G7 — Iris's research packet

**Form/find:** T bench satchel, already cleared for inspection as relevant papers. Dated photocopies and cover email show the signed receipt of the engineer's submission, refused-review correspondence, Adrian's 15 October offer to describe an omission as a clerical error, Ruth's request for an unqualified signed account before the deposit, and Iris sending copies three days ago. Receipt proves possession, not the full private suppression decision. It is discoverable paperwork, not the unrecorded final confrontation. **Objective:** dated information exchange and plausible historical grievance, not a witnessed suppression or murder. **Misreading:** Iris and Ruth plotted the murder, or every correspondent knew the truth in 2009. **Explanations:** Iris I-P admits copying; Ruth R-H discusses Elias/petition but not the killing. **Use:** optional deep motive/why-now context and explanation of Iris's unrelated concealment. G6 + ordinary R-H suffice for a limited motive explanation; both copy and original derive from the same historical statement, counted once. Neither must be found for a supported accusation on the opportunity/custody routes.

## G8 — repayment demand

**Form/find:** B tool tin, Adrian's folded note: "£480 from the maintenance fund is to be restored before tomorrow's handover. We discuss it 20:25." Owen has kept it rather than destroyed it. **Objective:** financial conflict and deadline, not theft classification or violent intent. **Misreading:** a strong motive plus a mother lying means murder. **Explanations:** Owen O1; Mara can speak about fear only after her gate, not identify the account. **Use:** optional independent path to O1 with G4, clarifies red herring. G4 + delivered M1 + grounded presentation is the alternative O1 gate. Work alibi and later living boundary remain obtainable without O1/G8. No assertion that the spending was secretly authorized after all.

## Clue economy and limitations

There are exactly eight named inspection items, distributed 2/1/2/1/2 across R/C/B/F/T. G1 is a coherent scene inspection; G3 a coherent service record; G4 a work packet. Do not expand these into surprise sub-inspections required after a player has already inspected them. Every description supplies its relevant visible facts on first inspection. No clue alone proves murder. G1/G2/G6 alone can justifiably raise Ruth as a strong early suspect, but do not establish a death window, actual appointment attendance, or account corroboration. Do not hide the Bell name or delay physical descriptions to protect a surprise; test those hypotheses instead. Both published routes omit G2/G7/G8; route A additionally omits G1, route B omits G5. Shared G3/G4/G6 are acknowledged dependencies, not a claim that any arbitrary three clues suffice. Future difficulty testing must check whether their bundling makes the puzzle too easy or too text-heavy.
