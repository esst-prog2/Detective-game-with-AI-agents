# Three mystery concepts

**Historical concept-selection sketches, preserved.** On 2026-11-06 the user selected A, The Last Bell at Greyhaven, and approved its foundation for detailed design only. B/C remain unselected alternatives. Exact particulars below are not approved canon; developer spoilers never enter model context or player payloads. The sketches themselves have no full timeline or graph; the current proposed detailed design begins in [greyhaven-story-bible.md](greyhaven-story-bible.md). Cast counts exclude the victim.

## A. The Last Bell at Greyhaven

### Premise, people and places

A former lifeboat station has become a small coastal guesthouse. At a farewell gathering for retired magistrate Adrian Vale, the ferry bell carries across the harbor. Adrian is later found dead after a fatal assault in the old chart room. Family members describe the evening relative to the bell, but they do not necessarily mean the same ringing or the same stretch of time. No supernatural device, medical trivia or hidden passage is needed.

Five living interviewees plus Adrian: six significant characters. Five purposeful locations:

| Location | Investigative purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Reception room | Establish relationships and distinguish what was observed from what was repeated |
| Chart room | Scene, documentary context and a physical trace needing interpretation |
| Boathouse | Test an apparent incriminating trace against ordinary work and an alternative account |
| Ferry office | Inspect the meaning and reliability of the evening's time reference |
| Upper terrace | Establish a witness's limited line of sight and clarify an ambiguous observation |

| Proposed character | Relationship and reason their account matters |
| --- | --- |
| Mara Vale | Adrian's sister and Owen's mother; knows Owen visited Adrian and fears what that means |
| Owen Vale | Mara's adult son, a boat mechanic; openly argued with Adrian and is hiding an unrelated wrongdoing |
| Iris Vale | Adrian's estranged daughter; sympathetic to Owen, distrustful of Mara and pursuing her father's old records |
| Ruth Bell | Retired nurse and trusted family friend; appears helpful and has a personal connection to Adrian's past |
| Jonah Reed | Ferry operator and Mara's former partner; understands the bell's operation but cannot vouch for everything family members infer from it |

**Developer-only proposed culprit/motive:** Ruth deliberately killed Adrian over his suppression of evidence in her brother's wrongful-conviction case and his threatened destruction of records she wanted restored. This motive and her relevant access must become investigable after story selection; it cannot be an ending-only revelation. Her helpfulness or professional role is not a guilt cue.

**Central deception:** Mara supplies a protective alibi for Owen. She knows about the visit, privately fears he may be responsible, and chooses to conceal it. Owen is innocent of the murder, though not of every wrongdoing. The player must separate a false alibi, an unrelated secret and the reliability of a shared time reference. The bell cannot function as a surprise trick: its ordinary operating limitations must be discoverable early enough to reconsider accounts.

**Three secret seeds, not a full inventory:** Mara conceals Owen's visit; Owen used money entrusted to him for an unauthorized repair; Iris passed restricted family records to an outside researcher. Ruth's concealed connection and murder are separate solution-side material. These facts would need explicit observation/provenance and disclosure review before use.

**Red herrings and fair explanations:** Owen's quarrel/false alibi make him a reasonable initial suspect; his repair activity could explain a trace that initially looks incriminating. Iris's secret meeting concerns the records, not the killing. The later design must independently corroborate those alternatives; an NPC's reassurance alone is insufficient. Ruth needs a discoverable, discriminating inconsistency beyond merely also having a motive.

### Investigative promise and behavior

Principal challenge: reconstruct relative events across sources, distinguish concealment from murder, and weigh a protective witness without forcing a confession. A proposed core question is "What did Mara actually see, and what did she infer afterward?" A promising pivot is Mara giving a more precise account when the player demonstrates an independently supported alternative to Owen's apparent involvement. A second route must reveal the essential observation if she remains guarded.

| Design principle | Application and constraint |
| --- | --- |
| A: truth/knowledge/belief/testimony | Owen's innocence is developer truth; Mara knows the visit, suspects guilt and gives a false alibi. Her fear is a belief, not a truth flag. |
| B: relationships | Mother/son loyalty determines the chosen account; Iris's distrust changes which witness the player seeks for corroboration. |
| C: motivated lies | Protection, unauthorized money use and document leakage explain different evasions; none automatically identifies the killer. |
| D: investigation | Compare the visit, an observable work trace and the bell reference. At least two source routes must support each essential inference in the later graph. |
| E: purposeful behavior | Evidence-based reassurance and an attributed contradiction can lead to authored revision; aggression changes delivery, never permanently removes the route. |
| F: clarification | Clarify which bell, whom a pronoun refers to, whether a sighting was direct, and the limits of the witness's certainty. No extra observation may be invented. |

Why richer than Ashcroft: the most conspicuous liar protects an innocent person; understanding why the lie exists helps reconstruct opportunity. The accusation would need timing/access plus independently supported connections, not a single personally marked item or incriminating contract. This remains a promising structure, not a demonstrated solvable case.

Complexity: **medium, lowest of these three**. One protective pair, one mistaken inference and two bounded testimony revisions can demonstrate the new model. Risk: a bell puzzle becomes an unfair twist or repeats one mechanical explanation across everyone. Mitigation: plain-language early inspection, mixed non-time evidence, and an independent observation route. A clock simulation or precise forensic death-time model is unnecessary.

## B. The Glass Dividend

### Premise, people and places

A worker-owned botanical glasshouse is opening a new export line. Founder Leona Saye is found dead in the loading court after an apparent fall from an inspection platform. An entry log points toward an outspoken maintenance engineer, while the cooperative's survival depends on keeping several financial problems quiet. The player must ask what a record actually proves: an active credential is not necessarily its owner's physical presence.

Six living interviewees plus Leona: seven significant characters. Five locations: public atrium (public claims/loyalties), loading court (scene and route observations), glasshouse platform (access and visibility), maintenance workshop (work practices and equipment records), cooperative office (entry records and finances). These are static explorable locations, not an environmental physics puzzle.

| Proposed character | Relationship and pressure |
| --- | --- |
| Anika Sen | Finance officer; Ravi's sister; popular for keeping members' wages paid |
| Ravi Sen | Maintenance engineer; loudly opposed Leona's shortcuts and has borrowed/shared access credentials |
| Eve Saye | Victim's daughter and spokesperson; publicly defends the expansion and privately depends on it |
| Tomas Finch | Dispatch lead and member representative; protected coworkers through an unrelated dishonest claim |
| Celia Moss | Bookkeeper financially dependent on the cooperative; knows accounting details without knowing the murder |
| Milo Hart | Visiting auditor with a concealed past working relationship with Leona |

**Developer-only proposed culprit/motive:** Anika killed Leona to prevent exposure of her diversion of member funds and the collapse of the benevolent identity she built around rescuing the cooperative. Exact method, access sequence and incriminating links remain to be designed. Financial expertise must not be required of the player; relevant transactions would be explained in-game.

**Central deception:** a shared/borrowed credential and a record's ambiguous event meaning turn Ravi's admitted procedural wrongdoing into apparent murder opportunity. Some colleagues defend him because of loyalty or labor politics; others repeat the log as if it were a witness. Anika's helpful financial narrative conceals a different interest. A log alone cannot convict or exonerate anyone.

**Three secret seeds:** Ravi concealed unauthorized credential sharing; Tomas made a false insurance claim to recover coworkers' lost wages; Eve pledged family property for the expansion without telling other members. The auditor's prior connection is an optional substitute, not a fourth mandatory subplot. Anika's diversion is solution-side material.

**Red herrings:** Ravi's argument and credential use are real but not sufficient; Tomas's false claim concerns a separate event; Eve's interest explains concealment without establishing scene presence. Each needs a reachable resolution through records plus independent observation. Avoid a late "someone hacked the log" escape: the ordinary credential semantics must be visible from the start.

### Investigative promise and behavior

Principal challenge: disentangle conflicting financial incentives, physical opportunity and what machine records measure. Two sources quoting the same log must not count as independent corroboration. Celia could disclose an accounting discrepancy after seeing a legitimate record, while Ravi revises a credential account when the player establishes its use for unrelated work. These are authored evidence reactions, not model deductions.

| Design principle | Application and constraint |
| --- | --- |
| A | Celia knows a payment trail but believes it protected wages; Ravi knows who borrowed access, not what happened during every use. |
| B | Sibling loyalty and shared livelihood affect accounts; witnesses can defend the same person for different reasons. |
| C | Unauthorized access, insurance dishonesty and hidden collateral provide intelligible non-murder lies. |
| D | Explain log semantics, compare physical access observations and follow plain-language money records through alternative routes. |
| E | A verified entry discrepancy can change Ravi's account; threatening jobs can produce a guarded alternative without locking the case. |
| F | "Whose card?", "Did you see the person?", "Was that entry or a machine operation?" and "How do you know who authorized it?" clarify distinct ambiguities. |

Why richer: several plausible material interests overlap, and the strongest-looking record must be interpreted rather than treated as infallible identification. The culprit need not be the person who argues most or admits the most procedural wrongdoing.

Complexity: **medium-high**. Extra authoring for shared-record provenance, access semantics and intelligible finances; the main risk is a bureaucratic spreadsheet puzzle. Suitable only if the eventual design can explain its essential deductions without outside accounting/engineering knowledge and without simulating credentials in real time.

## C. The Missing Refrain

### Premise, people and places

A restored hilltop radio station hosts a reunion honoring an old mountain rescue. Historian Elin Varga, preparing a revised exhibition, is found dead after an assault in a recording booth. Several guests tell the same confident story about the rescue and about the evening. Apparent corroboration begins to dissolve when the detective asks who directly observed each event and who learned it from someone else.

Six living interviewees plus Elin: seven significant characters. Five locations: exhibition hall (official story and relationships), recording booth (scene and witness limits), tape vault (source provenance), repair workshop (work records and an alternative activity), roof walkway (a partial sighting with restricted visibility). Old media is readable in a text transcript; audio playback/voice recognition is not required.

| Proposed character | Relationship and pressure |
| --- | --- |
| Hugo Neri | Celebrated former rescue captain whose authority shapes others' memories |
| Daria Holt | Former radio engineer; openly hostile to the exhibition and conspicuously evasive |
| Petra Ilic | Retired medic; grateful to Hugo and ashamed of an unrelated decision |
| Simon Reed | Former rescued child, now a teacher; emotionally invested in the public story |
| Luca Sera | Archivist and child of a missing radio operator; hopes Elin will correct the record |
| Nessa Pike | Station custodian and former dispatcher; remembers fragments but doubts herself |

**Developer-only proposed culprit/motive:** Hugo killed Elin to conceal his deliberate abandonment of a rescue group, preserve his celebrated identity and protect the institution built around it. His public leadership is not itself evidence. Daria, the conspicuous liar, is not the murderer. The eventual case must expose Hugo's relevant present opportunity and discriminating conduct as well as the past motive.

**Central deception:** accounts that sound independent are copied from a shared narrative. Some characters knowingly omitted details; others sincerely believe the inherited version. This is source dependence, not universal amnesia or an arbitrary false-memory reveal. Current-evening observations must remain separately recoverable so solving an old dispute is not mistaken for proving the new murder.

**Three secret seeds:** Daria altered an old transmission account to protect an operator she loved; Petra diverted rescue supplies for an unregistered patient; Luca has been leaking archive material. Hugo's hidden decision is solution-side material. These seeds are not a full account of the rescue or complete authorizations.

**Red herrings:** Daria's real record alteration explains her evasion without making her a killer; Petra's supply issue can be resolved as unrelated to Elin's death; Luca's anger and leaks explain meetings. A newly found tape must not contain a convenient murder confession or settle every disputed event. Old records and witness claims need explicit provenance and uncertainty.

### Investigative promise and behavior

Principal challenge: distinguish a repeated account from an independent observation, then connect present opportunity to a motive emerging from the past. Petra might revise a claim when shown that two accounts trace back to Hugo; Nessa might distinguish "I saw" from "I was told" when given a precise context. Nobody automatically gains private truth because another character was questioned.

| Design principle | Application and constraint |
| --- | --- |
| A | Simon sincerely believes a rescue story he did not witness fully; Daria knowingly changes one part; neither status determines current guilt. |
| B | Gratitude, an old romance, professional loyalty and inheritance of grief affect whom witnesses trust and protect. |
| C | Several omissions have non-murder explanations; significant diversions receive verifiable resolutions. |
| D | Trace sources, compare old records with firsthand limits, and independently establish the current murder's opportunity. No single tape solves both cases. |
| E | An attributed source challenge or exposed personal link can trigger a reviewed correction; anger never deletes the last path to an essential fact. |
| F | "Were you there?", "Who told you?", "Which rescue?" and "Are you recalling this or reading the exhibit?" reveal source/certainty limits, not fresh memories. |

Why richer: agreeing witnesses can still fail to corroborate, sincere error differs from a lie, and resolving one person's secret can change how another account is interpreted. The investigator must reason about reliability as well as access and motive.

Complexity: **high, greatest of the three**. Two historical contexts and many dependent sources make authoring and proof checking expensive. Risk: an exposition-heavy backstory swallows the present murder. A reduced version would keep only one disputed past incident and explicitly avoid modeling unrestricted memory, trauma, or a second fully playable investigation.
