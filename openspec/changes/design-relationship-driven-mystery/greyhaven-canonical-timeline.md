## Bell mechanism and public clock

Normally Jonah gives one **double peal at 20:30** to signal the final ferry's departure. Tonight he first pulls that signal at 20:30 while expecting the line to clear; the departure is then aborted. He records "20:30 signal; held at berth, mooring snag". At **20:42 he repeats the identical double peal** and the ferry departs. No mechanical fault, secret code or sound-propagation trick. The ordinary schedule is printed at the office; the delay notice and watchbook explain the exception. Jonah is the shore operator, not the ferry's master. The ferry crew handles the snag while he remains at the office from 20:32; the bell rope is inside beside the hatch. He does not leave Owen unwatched to clear the line or ring the later bell. Jonah supplies a plain-language explanation independently of any protected family account. The log and Jonah's explanation share an author, so are not two independent witnesses.

Everybody nearby can hear both peals, but hearing them does not show which one a witness meant by 'the last bell'. The mother hears both; she deliberately uses the interval for her alibi. Iris initially calls the first 'the ferry bell' because that is the usual departure time, not because she forgot twelve minutes. Ruth exploits the ambiguity; Jonah always distinguishes aborted signal from actual departure when asked. Owen hears both from different places. The public G3 record contains both times from the office clock; no requirement to synchronize a phone, wristwatch or weather report.
