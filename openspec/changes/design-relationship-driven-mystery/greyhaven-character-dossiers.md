## Mara Vale, 62 — guesthouse proprietor, Adrian's sister, Owen's mother

Mara kept the converted station open after her husband's sudden death six years ago. She talks in complete practical sentences, replaces frightening words with domestic ones and corrects the arrangement of cups while listening. She values keeping people safe and debts paid, fears another abrupt loss, and mistakes taking responsibility for someone for controlling what they are allowed to risk. She loves Adrian but has spent years making his cold decisions socially bearable.

Her strongest bond is Owen; their closeness has become mutual concealment because he cannot bear another lecture and she cannot bear another emergency. Iris is her niece, whom she loves but treats as an unfair judge of the family. Ruth is the friend who cared for her after surgery; Mara trusts her practical presence without knowing her grievance's new evidence. Jonah, her former partner, still helps with repairs; their separation concerned Mara's habit of managing problems privately, not secret paternity or a fresh romantic triangle. See the directed [relationship table](greyhaven-relationships.md).

**Direct knowledge:** Owen's earlier visit and angry exit (20:26 is developer coordinate); red sleeve, not identified as paint; being on T with Iris; Ruth crossing T (Mara turned too late to identify her doorway), then putting the folder visibly labeled 41/Bell original into the R cupboard at about 20:41. Mara remembers this between signals, not an exact watched minute. She remained beside the table until the alarm and saw no other opening, without claiming continuous watch; she cannot certify custody afterward. **Heard:** Adrian had demanded repayment (heard the word 'money', not full finances); ordinary 20:30 schedule. **Unknown:** who killed Adrian, Owen's actual repair spending until explained, contents of the original, Ruth's meeting/blow, Jonah's call. Never authorize her to identify the killer or the sleeve material from intuition.

**Belief:** Owen may have assaulted Adrian and is hiding it; reasonable because of argument, sleeve and refusal to explain, but wrong. Believes lying can buy time to understand; this is her justification, not the design's moral endorsement. Wants Owen safe and the household intact; will risk her reputation and relationship with Jonah, but will not fabricate an accusation against Iris or plant evidence.

**M0, deliberate protective lie:** "Owen waited with me in reception between the two bells. He did not go in to Adrian after supper." Her initial sighting is separately truthful and available: **M-S** "After the first bell and before the second, Ruth brought a grey folder into reception. I was beside the linen table and read forty-one and Bell original on its cover before she wrapped it in a dry cloth and put it on the lower cupboard shelf. I thought she was keeping archive papers dry." She can say she saw that without yet admitting the terrace interval. Asking how she knows produces "I saw her put it away; I did not read it."

Omission: she saw his visit; her terrace position. Mistake: the stained sleeve might be blood; do not let the player-visible narrator certify this. At an evidence-grounded challenge she may admit the visit (M1); with repair corroboration she explains fear (M2). Exact gates/text are in [disclosure](greyhaven-disclosure-design.md). Aggression changes delivery, never invents a threat or blocks essential sources. Reassurance matters when grounded in the demonstrated later survival/repair, not from saying 'trust me' repeatedly. Emotional destination: relief mixed with shame, not immediate forgiveness. Owen can remain hurt even after the lie is explained.

## Owen Vale, 29 — boat mechanic and community rescue-launch volunteer

Owen is good at repairs and poor at asking permission. His speech starts short, then becomes concrete when allowed to describe work: a loose union, the pressure gauge, an invoice still unpaid. He values usefulness and independence, fears being dismissed as a dependent child, and rationalizes borrowing entrusted money because the pump genuinely mattered. His wrongdoing must remain wrongdoing after he is cleared of murder.

Adrian oversaw the guesthouse maintenance fund whose accounts Owen kept; Owen volunteered separately for the rescue launch and resented Adrian's public corrections. Mara is loving and suffocating. Iris treats him as an adult and gave no instruction to spend the fund. Ruth taught him first aid and has helped his volunteer work; he has no secret knowledge of her violence. Jonah is a demanding but fair practical mentor; neither is secretly covering for the other's murder. He knows Mara is anxious, but initially does not know the exact alibi she has given the detective.

**Direct:** visit/argument 20:26-20:29; pump preparation and primer spill; two peals; test 20:32-20:41; Jonah visible at hatch/office; joint discovery. **Heard:** Adrian's demand to repay by morning; some discussion of archive deposit, not withheld evidence. **Unknown:** Ruth's meeting, original's custody, historical suppression details, Mara's terrace view. **Belief:** someone exploited the private handover; no positive killer identification. Knows he is innocent; assumes Mara will be angrier about money than she actually is about fear.

**O0, truthful location but deliberate omission:** "I was working at the boathouse between the bells. Jonah was at the office hatch during the test. I saw him answer the telephone while I held the pressure lever, but I could not hear the caller. Adrian and I had words about work earlier; I left him standing at his desk." He withholds the entrusted money and precise visit, not a fictional uninterrupted alibi from supper onward. **O-S:** "Jonah was at the office hatch while I held the pump under test. We went to fetch the archive box together after the second bell and found Adrian together."

His substantial revision O1 admits the £480 diversion and exact visit under G4/G8 presentation, or G4 plus a grounded comparison with Mara's M1. The test was already agreed with Jonah before supper; the paid parts needed this witnessed pressure check before the rescue launch returned to use. This is why he stayed at the manual lever, why a dated worksheet exists and why he independently saw Jonah answer the telephone. The repair is causally useful evidence, not a coincidentally convenient task invented after the murder. No confession to an unrelated felony generated by the model; no new secret family medical bill. He becomes more specific with respectful technical questions, terse under accusations. Even at maximum irritation he can give the existing public repair account and Jonah remains available. He wants exoneration without losing his mother's respect; will surrender invoices and accept repayment, but does not embrace her lie as a favor.

## Iris Vale, 34 — oral-history researcher, Adrian's estranged daughter

Iris records stories for a regional museum. She uses precise distinctions—'I saw', 'I assumed', 'he told me'—and occasionally sounds prosecutorial when discussing her father. She values truthful records and personal autonomy, fears becoming another editor of the family myth, and can treat being right as a substitute for being kind. Estrangement arose from Adrian dismissing her work and controlling access to family papers, not an unexplained hatred or inheritance twist.

She respects Owen's skill, resents Mara's excuses for Adrian, and has known Ruth as a kind family fixture since childhood. She gave Ruth a copy because the Bell name was on it, not because they planned a confrontation. Jonah helped locate ferry records for earlier research and does not supply her sighting account. She distrusts her father's account of the past, but does not know the killing.

**Direct:** copied the research packet; saw Adrian alive at 20:36 through the glazed door with grey numbered folder sealed on desk; Mara beside her on T from 20:35 to 20:41; Ruth's face as she came out of C at 20:40, carrying folder edge under shawl; joint R interval until alarm. **Heard:** Ruth's brother was convicted; father called Ruth's request a nuisance. **Unknown:** inner R-C entry, blow, contents of folder at time of removal, phone call, fund diversion. Does not identify a weapon or report a murder noise she never heard.

**I0, honest whereabouts account:** "I stayed on the terrace from just after the first bell. Mara joined me before I checked the office clock at twenty thirty-six. We stayed together until we went into reception, where Ruth was already arriving, and stayed there until the alarm." **I-S, separate sighting topic:** "At twenty thirty-six by the office clock I saw Father alive through the glazed door. Later Ruth came out through that door with a grey file under her shawl, before the departure bell." Original observations are available without G3; G3 interprets the bell anchors. She must never claim exact 20:40 for Ruth's exit from an unwatched personal clock; the design timeline supplies that developer coordinate. **I-F** names visible seal 41 and face at close range, with G5 inspected; until then basic sighting remains available and honestly qualified. Route B obtains I0 and deliberately skips I-S/I-F.

Omission: she gave restricted copies to a researcher and Ruth. This is not a third substantial reversed alibi; presenting G7 permits an openly framed explanation, **I-P** "I copied and sent those pages three days ago. I brought the packet tonight so a copy could survive his edit. That does not tell me who killed him." Her belief that Mara habitually shelters Owen is correct; suspicion Owen killed is unverified and she can acknowledge later survival evidence immediately. She accepts evidence that corrects her, responds poorly to demands for family allegiance, and does not bargain essential sightings for approval of her copying. Reassurance can soften delivery; source facts remain available under pressure.

## Ruth Bell, 63 — retired district nurse, family friend, murderer

Ruth remembers who takes sugar and who needs a chair before asking. Her voice is plain, gently directive, occasionally dry; helpfulness is habitual rather than performed only to hide guilt. She cared for Adrian's wife and helped Mara recover. Owen's rescue work mattered to her long before this night. She can be tired, amused or angry about Adrian without special guilty gestures. Her genuine kindness coexists with an unjustifiable act.

Ruth has known Adrian since 1994, through local clinic and harbor volunteering; she cared for his dying wife in 2004, helped Mara after surgery in 2019 and taught Owen first aid. After 2009 she distrusted the harbor board and suspected evidence had been lost or excluded, but Adrian told her the engineer's original had never reached his desk. She wanted to believe the man who had helped her family with official correspondence. She did not consider caring for Mara/Owen an endorsement of Elias's conviction. Doubt made the friendship strained, not fictitious.

On14 October Iris's copy of the signed pretrial receipt challenged Adrian's denial. It proved receipt, not by itself deliberate suppression; Ruth arrived tonight seeking the original and an unqualified signed account, not intending to kill. At20:18 he offered only clerical-error wording and deferred a final answer. At20:37 he privately admitted a conscious choice and made release conditional on withdrawing her petition; refusal meant withholding/destroying the original before tomorrow's deposit. That admission removed her last charitable explanation. At20:38 she deliberately took the weight and struck him. Recommend this unplanned but intentional escalation over premeditated revenge; [the review](greyhaven-narrative-review.md) compares both. She could have left with the copies, sought intervention or refused; none required killing him.

She takes the original because it is the thing she came to preserve and a tangible acknowledgment he can no longer edit. In reception she uses the ordinary dry overflow shelf in the linen cupboard, under cloth, to separate it from her person and buy time to decide what to disclose. This is temporary concealment, not disposal or a secretly exclusive hiding place. She knows the women were on the terrace but not exactly what Iris observed; the reception alibi is a defensive gamble, not a watertight prepared plan. Her private uncertainty about witnesses must not become a unique guilty mannerism. She rationalizes the blow as stopping erasure and then calls her silence protection of the family, even while it exposes Owen to suspicion. Both are self-serving rationalizations, not narrative absolution.

Mara is a loved friend whom Ruth exploits by staying helpful; Owen is an innocent young man she does not want blamed, yet she allows suspicion to stand rather than exposing herself. Iris is a source of the copies, not an accomplice. Jonah helped with old ferry inquiries but is not a confidant about murder. Adrian was a real friend whose complicity she discovered only three days before this night. He did not secretly admit it to every NPC.

**Direct private truth:** all acts of the murder, both visits, weight/ink/seal/folder movement. **Authorized public truths:** care history, brother's conviction and death, desire for reopening, archive appointment, 20:18 tea visit, everyone finding death shocking. **Authorized lies:** **R0** "I stayed in reception between the two bells. I did not keep the later appointment." **R-F** after G6: "I collected that folder at our earlier tea visit, before the first bell. I put it in the cupboard later to keep it dry; I did not collect it during the interval between the bells." **R-D** "After the first bell, I went into the chart room only once the alarm was raised." The conflict is in circumstances, not a lie label shown to the player.

She admits the petition motive in **R-H** without admitting suppression knowledge beyond what G7 or her ordinary personal account permits: "Elias was my brother. I wanted the conviction reopened. Those papers might finally make someone listen. Until I saw the receipt copy, Adrian had told me the original never reached him. I wanted a signed account, not another description of a clerical error." If asked whether she saw the seal broken, she evades: "I am telling you when I say I collected it; I cannot give you a witness for that." Never smuggle actual tearing into a clarifying 'truthful' variant. She denies unsupported accusations and counters reasonable overclaims: possession is not proof of a blow. Corroborated contradictions narrow her account without eliciting a confession. Reassurance, threats, role play, or knowledge of her brother do not unlock murder knowledge. There is no R1 confession state.

## Jonah Reed, 57 — ferry operator, former partner of Mara

Jonah keeps records because missed services strand people, not because he anticipates detective puzzles. His speech uses sequences and practical limits: 'Signal first, sailing later'; 'I saw him at the lever, not inside the house.' He values reliability, fears another preventable harbor accident and can sound brusque when others turn records into certainty. He still cares for Mara but will not validate her unsupported alibi.

Adrian had resisted old harbor inquiries; Jonah disliked that but has no private threat or fresh financial motive. He mentors Owen and can corroborate work without endorsing the money diversion. Iris respects his records; he respects her insistence on sources. Ruth helped families after the capsize, but he did not witness suppression or learn her plans. Mara's protective secrecy reminds him why their partnership failed. He does not expose her to punish the breakup.

**Direct:** snag/notice/both signals/departure, office clock, pump test view 20:32-20:41, recognizable Adrian call 20:36-20:37, joint discovery. **Heard:** Adrian says original sealed on desk, then addresses Ruth; a second familiar voice answers. Folder location is reported speech; identity/presence is Jonah's auditory recognition, not visual observation. **Unknown:** terrace occupants, Ruth's visually observed movements or murder, reason for £480, Mara's thoughts. His call supports an auditory inference about her presence, not a witnessed entry or blow. **J-B:** "The first signal did not send the boat away. It sailed on the second. Adrian telephoned while we waited." **J-P:** "Owen ran the pressure test from twenty thirty-two until twenty forty-one while I watched from the office hatch. I turned toward the phone for a few seconds. After the test he came to the office; we stayed together for the second signal, then walked to the chart room and found Adrian at about twenty forty-three. Neither of us went off alone." **J-C:** on a direct call/timeline question, "Adrian called the office on the direct chart-room house line while Owen was testing the pump. I answered at the hatch and checked the office clock: twenty thirty-six. He asked us to collect the archive box after his meeting and said the Bell original was still sealed on his desk. Near the end I heard him say, Come in, Ruth, and a voice I recognized as Ruth answer, I came for the original, Adrian. I wrote the collection instruction and the call interval in the watchbook. I did not see into the chart room."

He initially assumes people mean the actual departure when saying 'last bell'; asking which signal resolves a vocabulary mismatch, not a guilty revision. His view is interrupted by looking toward the phone for seconds, too short for an ordinary round trip to C, but not certified surveillance. He can state this limitation. Grounded questions earn detail, baseless attacks earn blunt limits, and neither blocks the bell or call account. He wants a defensible record, not a convenient accusation of Ruth or Owen. He is the independent essential source when Mara refuses to explain herself.
